Governance

How {{ org_short }} makes decisions, controls money and stays accountable to members.

Decision-making

Leadership sets direction and approves policies. Important decisions are recorded in meeting minutes, and members can take part through meetings and votes.

Financial controls

  • Every contribution, project payment, expense, revenue item and distribution is recorded in a double-entry ledger.
  • Balances are calculated from recorded transactions and cannot be typed in by hand.
  • Money movements need approval by a second officer (two-person rule).
  • Payments count only after they are verified with M-PESA or the bank.
  • Mistakes are corrected with reversal entries; financial records are never deleted.
  • Bank and M-PESA balances are reconciled against statements regularly.

Project approval

Every project goes through review, financial review, risk assessment and approval before any money is released. Funding depends on approval and available funds; not every project is funded.

Member participation

Members can see their own records, attend meetings, vote where the rules allow, raise complaints and contact support.

Meetings

Meetings are announced on the member dashboard with an agenda. Attendance, minutes, decisions and follow-up actions are recorded.

Voting

Eligible members vote once per proposal. Votes are secret: only totals are shown. Results depend on the quorum and pass mark set for each vote.

Audit

  • An auditor role has read-only access to financial records, reports and the audit log.
  • Every important administrative action is written to an audit log (who, what, when, old and new values, reason). Entries cannot be changed or deleted.
  • Automatic checks raise alerts for human review — for example duplicate payment references or unusual activity.
  • Reconciliation and audit reports can be produced from the ledger at any time.

Complaints

Anyone can raise a complaint and track it with a reference number.

Conflicts of interest

  • Officers cannot approve transactions, payments or distributions they entered themselves.
  • Partners can report expenses and revenue but cannot verify them or approve their own funding.
  • A person who both proposes and manages a project cannot approve it.
  • Anyone with a personal interest in a decision should declare it and step aside from that decision.

Transparency

Summary figures, project results and published reports are available on the Transparency Center, without any private member information.
